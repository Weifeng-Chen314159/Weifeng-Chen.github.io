Research Interest

My research explores the principles of molecular self-organization across scales, from the origins of life to cellular biophysics. I combine quantitative modeling and experiment to study how ordered structures and functions emerge from disordered systems, with a focus on phase separation, protocell assembly, and stress-driven adaptation. I am also interested in biotechnological applications.

Mineral-guided molecular enrichment: An interfacial driving force for protocell emergence on early Earth
WeiFeng Chen, Rongfeng Zheng, Shiyuan Pei, Guoquan Wu, Jiayu Lei, Zhaosen Luo, Haochun Gou, Hao Li, Chunhui Li, Guozhi Yu
The emergence of protocells from dilute prebiotic environments is constrained by the dilution paradox, whereby low molecular concentrations hinder the assembly of metabolic and compartmentalized systems. Here we show that mineral-guided surface enrichment (MSE) provides a geochemically plausible mechanism to address this constraint.

Cooperativity-Induced Bifurcation and Phase Separation in Stress-Driven RNA–Peptide Systems
WeiFeng Chen, RongFeng Zheng, GuoZhi Yu*
Environmental stress can act as a selective pressure that drives cooperative coupling in RNA–peptide mixtures. We develop a minimal coevolutionary model in which stress simultaneously accelerates degradation and favors protective heterotypic interactions. Through controlled adiabatic elimination of fast population and trait variables, we obtain a one-dimensional dynamical equation for a dimensionless coupling coordinate X. The model reveals a cooperativity-induced saddle-node bifurcation that occurs only when the protection kernel has Hill exponent n>1. Below a critical stress ηc, the system resides in a low-coupling state; above ηc, it switches discontinuously to a high-coupling branch with asymptotic scaling X* ∼ (n A η)1/(n+1). This bifurcation is accompanied by complete decoupling between functional evolution (slow) and total molecular abundance (fast), a generic consequence of timescale separation under resource limitation. We further relate X to an effective Flory–Huggins interaction parameter through a bounded monotone mapping, showing that the dynamical jump can bring the mixture close to a mean-field spinodal for liquid–liquid phase separation. Stochastic Monte Carlo simulations of a particle-based RNA–peptide system confirm that the reduced description captures the dominant coarse-grained dynamics. Our results demonstrate how cooperative feedback can convert a continuously increasing stress into a qualitative transition in both molecular function and mesoscale organization.
